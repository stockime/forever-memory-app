# The Road South to the Sepulcher

Deathguard Burgess wanted proof that fewer of the Scarlet Watch Post were watching us. I brought him their rings. It is an ugly sort of proof, a man's signet pulled from his hand, but the Crusade had been watching Brill closely, and Brill has people in it I would rather keep. "The Dark Lady will hear of it," he said. I have now been told that so often today that I expect she is weary of my name.

From there the road ran south into Silverpine, under trees that drip even when it isn't raining, to the Sepulcher. Shari Stilwell had gone ahead of me somehow. She taught me something new and greeted me with the very same words as before: "Light keep you, Forsaken. Few would have believed it." Either she says it to every one of us or she is still trying to convince herself. I did not ask which.

High Executor Hadrec sent me to the Dead Field. The Mudsnout shamans were calling on things best left buried, and a road out there needed clearing. After that Dalar Dawnweaver sent me on to Malden's Orchard, with no more reason than that words are cheap in the Sepulcher and I ought to prove myself. I put down moss stalkers and Rot Hide plague weavers until the orchard smelled of nothing but rot. Proving myself to a man who plainly does not care seems a strange use of a shield. I did it anyway. The forest was full of those beasts and gnolls, and they found me whether I went looking or not.

Then Rane Yorick told me of Ivar the Foul, a farmer once, now a butcher who keeps worgen hounds and "remembers everyone who wronged him." Ivar killed me. It was his cleaver and his dogs, and then the cold grey quiet I know better than I should. I came back from it and walked in on him again, and it was not a quick business. In the end his hounds lay in the mud, and so did he. Yorick gave me a pair of Deathguard pauldrons for his head. They sit well. Ivar's remembering is over. I wonder who will remember him instead.

Once again I bled a river and asked the Light for a trickle. It came each time I asked. I keep waiting for the day it won't, and I think that waiting is heavier than any pauldron.

Other travellers passed me on these roads. None of them stayed long. There is nobody I can add to my count of the living tonight, and that sits poorly with me. The Wolfsbane Oath is still unkept, and somewhere past these trees Arugal waits in his keep. Tomorrow I will lean further south, and I am stronger than I was this morning.
