# The First Day Behind the Shield

I woke in Deathknell with dirt in my mouth and a Light in my chest I had no right to. Undertaker Mordo said I did not rise the same as the others. He meant it kindly, I think. Hard to tell with Mordo.

Shadow Priest Sarvis sent me looking for a frightened paladin among the risen, "before the others decide what to do with him." I found him clutching his hammer like a drowning man. I did not tell him I was every bit as frightened. It seemed a poor thing to hand a man who was already sinking.

The Executor and the Deathguard kept me busy after that. I put the mindless dead back in the ground and broke the Rattlecage bones that still answer to the Lich King. It was grim work, not proud work. They were us, once, or near enough.

The task that stays with me came from Aramis Hammerhand: "not even death can stop the Light." He sent me into Night Web's Hollow to cut the risen free of the spiders' silk. That I understood. Every one I pulled loose was one more walking out of that dark on their own feet. I will keep that count long after I forget the rest.

Then the Scarlet Crusade, camped at Solliden Farmstead within sight of our chapel, praying to the same Light I carry and swinging at me for carrying it. I did not enjoy that. I did it anyway.

The road east brought me to Brill, and to Shari Stilwell, who looked at me a long while and said, "Light keep you, Forsaken. Few would have believed it." I am not sure I believe it either. She taught me a little more of the craft all the same.

Brill wanted its errands done. There was gloom weed for old Gordo, who can't carry much any more. There were pumpkins from the fields by Brightwater Lake, gathered while I drove the Rot Hide gnolls off the graves they kept digging up. Last came the Agamand Mills, full of Scourge bones walking the halls of a proud family. Coleman Farthing wanted them cleared, and they are, for now.

I bled a great deal today and called on the Light to mend me hardly at all. Some of that was stubbornness. Some of it, if I am honest, was fear that I would ask and nothing would come.

Other travellers crossed my road, and there are oaths still hanging over me that I have not yet kept. Tomorrow, then. The shield is heavier than it was this morning, and I have grown enough to carry it.
